SAMQUANT PHASE 5 - VERY SIMPLE EXPLANATION

WHAT DOES PHASE 5 DO?

Phase 5 is SamQuant's report card.

After SamQuant tests a trading strategy, Phase 5 studies the results and answers:

1. Did the strategy make or lose money?
2. How risky was the strategy?
3. How bad was its biggest fall?
4. Were its completed trades usually winners or losers?


WHAT DOES PHASE 5 ADD?

1. TOTAL RETURN

This shows the overall percentage gained or lost.

Example:
The portfolio starts with $100 and ends with $120.
Total return = 20%.


2. ANNUALIZED RETURN

This turns the result into an estimated average return per year.

This helps us compare tests that lasted for different amounts of time.

Example:
A 6-month test and a 2-year test cannot be compared fairly using only their
total returns. Annualized return puts both results on a yearly scale.


3. VOLATILITY

This measures how much the portfolio value jumps up and down.

Small jumps usually mean a smoother journey.
Large jumps usually mean more uncertainty and risk.


4. SHARPE RATIO

This compares the strategy's extra return with the risk it took.

A larger Sharpe ratio usually means the strategy earned more return for each
unit of risk. It does not prove that the strategy will succeed in the future.


5. MAXIMUM DRAWDOWN

This finds the biggest fall from a previous high point.

Example:
The portfolio grows to $150 and later falls to $105.
The fall is $45, which is a 30% maximum drawdown.

It answers: "What was the worst drop an investor had to survive?"


6. WIN RATE

This shows the percentage of completed sell trades that made money.

SamQuant matches sold shares with earlier purchases using FIFO:
"first bought, first sold."

It also includes trading fees, because fees reduce the real profit.


WHAT DOES PHASE 5 CHECK?

- The equity history is present and usable.
- Portfolio values are valid numbers and greater than zero.
- Dates are in the correct order.
- There are enough observations for calculations that need multiple values.
- Settings such as periods per year are valid.
- Trades are in time order.
- A sell does not use more shares than were previously bought.
- Trading fees are included when deciding whether a trade won.

If a metric cannot honestly be calculated, SamQuant returns NaN, meaning
"not enough valid information." It does not pretend the answer is zero.


SMALL EXAMPLE

Suppose a strategy starts with $1,000:

- It finishes with $1,100.
- It reached $1,200 before falling to $900.
- It completed 10 sell trades.
- 6 of those trades made money after fees.

Phase 5 can report:

- Total return: 10%
- Maximum drawdown: 25% (from $1,200 to $900)
- Win rate: 60%
- Plus annualized return, volatility, and Sharpe ratio


HOW PHASE 5 FITS INTO SAMQUANT

Market data -> Strategy signals -> Simulated trades -> Portfolio history
-> Phase 5 analytics -> Clear performance report

Phase 5 does not choose trades and does not promise profits.
It honestly measures the results produced by the earlier phases.
